## Abstract

This research examines color as an autonomous, relational and
generative principle in the work of Sonia Delaunay.

It investigates the historical transformation of color theory
from Newton and Goethe through Chevreul and Seurat to the
simultaneist visual language developed by Sonia Delaunay.

The study focuses on the relationship between color, perception,
form, rhythm, spatial organization and temporal experience.

Particular attention is given to:

- Contrastes simultanés
- Bal Bullier
- Prismes électriques
- Textile and clothing design
- Simultaneism
- The relationship between painting and applied arts
- Textile matrices and structural repetition
- Digital images and algorithmic visual systems
- Shader-based visual structures

The central proposition is that color is not merely a property
of an existing form. It can function as an autonomous operation
through which visual form, space and rhythm emerge.

## Research Structure

### 01. Introduction and Research Framework

Historical and theoretical foundations of color as a relational
and perceptual phenomenon.

### 02. Development of the Research and Methodology

Historical-genealogical, relational, perceptual and intermedial
research methods.

### 03. Main Research Findings and Detailed Analysis

Examination of Delaunay's paintings, textile practice and the
structural relationships between simultaneism and digital imagery.

### 04. The Structural Development of the Concept

Development of the generative principle as a differentiated
theoretical model.

### 05. Conclusion and Scholarly Significance

The significance of color autonomy in modernism and its critical
relationship to contemporary visual systems.

### 06. Further Research Possibilities

Future research directions concerning color perception,
education, cultural mediation and artificial intelligence.
